Control row colour: four options

macOS Designer research note, 24 September 2026. Options only, no code change proposed yet. Every panel below is drawn at the medium scale using the real PanelScale numbers (480pt wide, 20pt controls, 16pt glyph box, 13pt glyph, 5pt radius, 14pt row gap, 7pt inside the mic group). SF Symbols cannot be loaded in a browser, so every glyph here is a hand drawn SVG approximation of the real symbol, not the symbol itself. Judge the colour, the fill and the rhythm; do not judge the glyph shapes.

First: the premise needs one correction

I read MacRecordWidgetApp.swift before writing this. The record controls are already red: the mic toggle and the video toggle both carry .tint(.red) (lines 68 and 110), and they already carry the blinking amber RecordingDot. Nothing about recording is blue today.

The blue you are reacting to is on the other four controls: the pin toggle, the mirror toggle, and the three S/M/L size toggles. Those have no .tint, so SwiftUI's .button toggle style fills them with controlAccentColor, which is blue by default and follows System Settings. Since mirror defaults to on, the pin is often on, and one size toggle is always on, the resting state of the panel is three to five blue rectangles and nothing else. That is the actual problem, and it reframes the question: this is not about how recording looks, it is about how settings look.

That distinction drives the recommendation at the bottom.

Option A · Accent Is The User's Choice

Change nothing. Every non capture toggle keeps controlAccentColor; the two capture toggles keep red. The argument is that accent colour is not a colour we picked, it is a colour the user picked in System Settings, and overriding it is the opposite of respecting the platform.

Precedent

  • SwiftUI's own .toggleStyle(.button) default. Verified by behaviour in this app: untinted toggles fill with accent.
  • Sidebar selection, checkbox fills and focus rings across the system are accent driven. From memory.

Trade offs

  • Accent respect: perfect, by definition. A graphite user gets a graphite panel; a pink user gets pink.
  • Colourblind: safe. Nothing here encodes meaning in hue that is not also encoded in the glyph.
  • Reduce Motion / Increase Contrast: nothing to do, the system handles it.
  • One control, two states: yes, this is the strongest option on that test. Same geometry, same outline, fill flips.
  • The actual complaint: unaddressed. At rest the panel is a cluster of blue rectangles with no hierarchy, and mirror (a preview preference) shouts exactly as loudly as the pin (a window behaviour). It is platform correct and visually flat.
Honest verdict: correct, and not what you asked for. Keep it on the list because "do nothing" is the option every other option has to beat.

Option B · Semantic Capsules (the FaceTime model)

Each function gets its own fixed colour on a fully rounded capsule, white glyph on top. Capture is red, camera and preview functions are green, window behaviour is indigo, preview size is teal. Accent colour is abandoned inside the row.

Precedent

  • FaceTime's camera control, white glyph on a green capsule. Your observation, which is the whole reason this option exists.
  • Control Center tiles: per function colour on a round pill. From memory, not verified.
  • Important caveat I am reasonably confident about: both of those are call and system controls with two to five items. This row has nine. The pattern may not survive the scale up, and this mock is the evidence either way.

Trade offs

  • Accent respect: none. A graphite user still gets a four colour row. That is defensible for one capture button, much less so for a size picker.
  • Colourblind: this is where it hurts. Green mirror sits two controls from red mic. Deuteranopia flattens exactly that pair, and here the two colours are the only difference in ON state, since every capsule is identical in shape. The glyphs still differ, which saves it from being unusable, but the colour is then carrying nothing.
  • Increase Contrast: needs four hand tuned high contrast variants, one per semantic colour, in both appearances. Eight values to maintain by hand, none of them semantic.
  • One control, two states: fails. The capsule changes the corner radius from 5pt to fully round, so an ON control is a visibly different shape from an OFF one. It also breaks the constant 1pt outline rule, which every control in the row currently shares.
  • Row reading: nine controls in four colours reads as a toy. This is the ui simplicity rubric's "hierarchy through decoration" failure: colour is doing work that grouping and spacing already do.
Honest verdict: the reference is real, the scale up is not. FaceTime colours one button in a window that has three. Colouring nine is a different design.

Option C · Quiet Chrome, Loud Capture

Split the row by kind, not by function. Settings toggles (pin, mirror, S/M/L) drop accent entirely and show ON as a recessed neutral fill: Color.primary at low opacity with an inner shadow, exactly the pressed in look AppKit gives a push on push off button. Only the two controls that capture something stay red. Colour becomes a signal that means one thing: something is being recorded.

Precedent

  • Finder's view mode and grouping toolbar toggles, Mail's sidebar toggle, Notes' formatting toggles: selection as a grey recess, not an accent fill. From memory, not verified. This is the single claim most worth you checking, because Option C rests on it.
  • Red as the capture signal: QuickTime, Screenshot, the system recording indicator. From memory. Also already true in this app, which is verified.
  • macOS HIG guidance that accent marks selection and interactive highlight. A preview size choice arguably is a selection, which is the honest counterargument to this option.

Trade offs

  • Accent respect: mixed, and worth arguing about. It removes accent from four controls. The defence: it does not replace accent with another hue, it replaces it with the label colour, so a graphite user and a pink user get the identical panel, and neither gets a panel that fights their choice. The attack: a user who set their accent to pink did so in order to see pink.
  • Colourblind: best of the four. Exactly one hue in the row, red, and it appears only while capturing. Nothing is distinguished by hue pairs at all. The red vs green problem never arises because green is never used.
  • Increase Contrast: free. The neutral fill is derived from labelColor, so it darkens with the system automatically. One value to tune, not eight.
  • One control, two states: passes cleanly. Same rect, same 5pt radius, same 1pt outline, fill and inner shadow flip. Arguably it passes better than today, since a grey recess reads as "this rectangle, pushed in" while a saturated blue fill reads as a different object.
  • Risk: ON versus OFF is a lower contrast difference than blue versus white. It must be checked on a real panel at the small scale, where the control is 16pt tall. If it is too quiet, the fix is more inner shadow, not more colour.
Honest verdict: this is the one that actually solves what you complained about, and it solves it by removing colour rather than adding it.

Option D · Live Ember (Option C plus a breathing capture state)

Option C's quiet chrome, plus the moving state you asked for. While capturing, the active control's fill is not flat red but a red to ember gradient that drifts diagonally on a 3.4s loop, with a soft outer glow breathing on the same period. The period is deliberately not the recording dot's 1.6s: two indicators pulsing at close but unequal rates look broken, so the gradient runs at slightly over double, and the two read as one slow breath with a faster heartbeat on the corner rather than as a stutter.

The two rows below are animating right now. Flip the Simulate Reduce Motion switch at the top right to see the static fallback, which is plain Color.red, identical to Option C.

Precedent

  • I know of no standard macOS control that animates its fill. Assertion from memory, and the weakest claim on this page, but I could not think of a counterexample. Siri's orb and the Screen Sharing / screen recording menu bar indicator are the closest, and both are status indicators, not controls.
  • That is the honest framing for this option: it is not a platform pattern, it is a deliberate, scoped departure. Per my own hard constraint I have to justify it rather than just draw it. The justification: this control is simultaneously a button and the app's only persistent "you are live" indicator, and the panel is frequently closed, so the indicator has to survive being glanced at. Motion survives a glance better than hue.
  • Counterargument you should weigh: the app already has a motion channel for exactly this, the blinking amber dot. Option D adds a second one. Two moving things on a 20pt control may be one too many.

Trade offs

  • Accent respect: same as C. Unaffected by animation.
  • Colourblind: same as C, and slightly better: motion is a non hue channel, so the recording state now has three independent signals (glyph fill, the dot, the drift).
  • Reduce Motion: mandatory static fallback, shown above. The app already has the pattern to copy: RecordingDot reads accessibilityReduceMotion and holds steady. The gradient must do the same and collapse to flat red, never to nothing.
  • Increase Contrast: the ember highlight at mid cycle is lighter than systemRed, so white glyph contrast dips for part of each loop. Under Increase Contrast the animation should be dropped, not just narrowed. That is a second static fallback and it must be specified, not inferred.
  • One control, two states: passes on geometry (same rect, same radius, same outline) but this is the one place where an ON control is no longer merely "the same control filled". If the row is meant to read as one family in two states, an animating member is a deliberate exception, made for the one control whose state is the app's entire purpose.
  • Cost: a drifting gradient repaints a small layer continuously for the whole recording. On a menu bar panel that is often closed this is near free, but it is not zero, and it should be paused when the panel is not visible.
Honest verdict: the animation earns its place only if it replaces a signal rather than stacking on one. My recommendation below says how.

Recommendation

Ship Option C, and take exactly one piece of Option D: the breathing gradient on the capture control, replacing the blinking amber dot on that control rather than joining it.

  • Why C over A: your complaint is real and A does not answer it. Accent respect is a good principle but it is being applied to the wrong controls. Accent means "selected or interactive highlight". A preview mirror preference and a window pinning preference are neither; they are persistent modes, and macOS draws persistent toolbar modes as a recess, not a flood of colour. C does not take the user's accent away from anything that should have had it, because none of those four controls should have had it.
  • Why C over B: B is the option you were pointing at, and I am recommending against it. FaceTime colours one button; this row has nine, and B makes four of them coloured capsules. It fails the "one control, two states" constraint outright by changing the corner radius, it puts green two controls away from red, and it costs eight hand maintained contrast values. The idea you liked in FaceTime is not "colour the buttons", it is "the important one is unmistakable". C delivers that by making it the only coloured thing in the row. B delivers the opposite, because when four things are coloured, none of them is the important one.
  • Why take D's motion, with a subtraction: the moving fill is the right instinct. The mistake would be adding it on top of the existing blink. Today the recording control carries red fill, a blinking dot and a running timer, which is already three signals on a 20pt square; a fourth turns it into a Christmas tree. So on the recording control, the gradient replaces the dot. The dot stays where it is genuinely needed and cannot be replaced: the menu bar icon, which is 16pt, always visible, and has no room for a gradient.
  • What I will not do without evidence: the Finder / Mail grey recess precedent that C rests on is from memory. Before this becomes a component spec, open a Finder window and a Mail window and confirm what their active toolbar toggles actually look like under a non blue accent. If they turn out to be accent filled, C loses its platform argument and we should talk again.
