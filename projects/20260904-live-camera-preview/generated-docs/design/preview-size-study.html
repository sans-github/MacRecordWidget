5 · Recommendation

Ship M — 320×180 preview, 344×256pt panel, 6.6% of screen.

The preview here is display-only: Photo Booth is the capturer. So the only job the preview has is “am I framed, lit, and pointed at the right camera?” — a glance, answered in about a second, then the user hits record. 320×180 answers that at normal viewing distance. It is also the smallest size where the camera picker and mirror toggle both fit as visible controls, which matters more than pixels: at S they have to hide in a menu, and a persisted setting the user cannot see is a setting they will get wrong.

L (480×270) is a defensible fallback if you sit far from the display or want to check fine detail like text on a whiteboard. 13% of screen is large for a menu bar extra but still reads as a panel, not a window. I would not pick it by default — it costs 2× the screen area to answer the same question.

XL (1090×614) should not ship. Being direct, since you asked for it: this is not a menu bar popover any more, it is a window wearing a popover’s corner radius. Three concrete problems, not just style:

  • It dismisses on any outside click. A MenuBarExtra(.window) panel is transient by design. The moment you click your browser, your slides, anything, a preview covering 57% of your screen vanishes. A surface that big invites the user to treat it as persistent, and it is not. That mismatch is the whole problem.
  • The HIG is explicit that a menu bar extra is a quick, transient control surface for lightweight always-available access — not a viewer. This is a departure I cannot justify on platform grounds, and I am flagging it rather than designing around it.
  • It buys nothing over L. Same two controls, same framing decision, 4.4× the screen area. Preview alone is 49.9% of the screen; with the control rows the panel is 57.3%.

If the real requirement behind XL is “I want to watch myself full size while recording,” that is a different feature and a different container: a separate resizable Window scene (“Camera Preview”, ⌥⌘P, floating level optional) that persists across clicks, remembers its frame, and can be moved to a second display. That is the native answer. It should be scoped as its own item rather than smuggled into the popover — I have not designed it here.

Panel width changes when the toggle flips. At M, L, or XL the panel grows from 200pt to the wider size the moment video is switched on, and shrinks back when off. That resize is visible and slightly jarring on every toggle. Two mitigations, both cheap: keep the panel at its wide width permanently (the row just gets more trailing space), or animate the change and gate it on accessibilityReduceMotion. Worth a decision at mock time — S is the only option that avoids the question entirely, which is its one real advantage.

Open questions for PM before final mocks: (1) does the panel stay wide when video is off, or resize; (2) is the persisted camera choice per-app or shared with Photo Booth’s own selection; (3) what shows in the preview area when camera permission is denied or no camera is present — that empty/error state is not covered in this study.
